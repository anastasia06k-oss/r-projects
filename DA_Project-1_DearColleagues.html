Problem Outline

For the first project we have chosen Italy from the European Social Survey dataset. Our scientific interest focuses on the relationship between Italians’ working life and their health. The research question can be formulated as how labor conditions affect workers’ health in Italy?

Data Filtering

Here we filtered our dataset, limiting it to only Italy-related records and variables of our interest. We also removed all specially encoded values which refer to different kinds of NAs (due to refusal, lack of answer and others, coded as ‘66’, ‘777’, ‘8888’ and so on). Also we renamed the variables with more intuitive names using rename() from dplyr for quicker navigation between them. The variables chosen include Internet usage, age, type of organization a person works at, subjective perception of the degree of control at work, subjective health evaluation, smoking habit, drinking habit, dietary habit (vegetables consumption) and working hours per week.

ess$cntry <- as.factor(ess$cntry)

  filter(cntry == 'IT')

For bar plots we transformed the data into a longer format so that it would fit facet_wrap layer nicely.

ess_italy %>%
  select(drinking, smoking, health, veggies, type_of_org) %>%
  pivot_longer(everything()) %>%

       x = "Factor's value",
       y = 'Frequency')

Findings: The picture shows that the majority of observations in the sample: work for private companies, eat vegetables twice a day, have never smoked, drink alcohol several times a week or once a week and consider their health to be generally good.

In this bar plot we are comparing the health level of the workers in different types of organizations. The x axis represents the type of organization, y axis is the number of observations and the columns are filled with the health level of the workers. Private firms have the largest amount of observations. By looking at the graph, we can say that the most workers feel good (level 2), fever amount of workers feel very good (level 3) and less people answered that they feel ‘fair’ (level 3). The other health levels ‘bad’ and ‘very bad’ are the lest common report in every type of organizations. Self-reported health level is moderate in all organizations, but the sample is heavily concentrated in ‘private firm’ type of organizations.

Interpretation: Boxplot

ess_italy %>%
  ggplot(aes(health_levels, total_work_per_week, fill = health_levels)) + 
  geom_boxplot(outlier.colour = 'gray') + 
  theme_minimal() + 
  theme(legend.position = 'none') +
  labs(
    title = 'Relationship between working hours per week and general health',
    x = 'Self-reported general health',
    y = 'Number of working hours per week',
    fill = 'Self-reported general health')

Using a boxplot we visualize the relationship between number of working hours per week, which is a numeric variable, and self-reported general health, which is an ordinal variable. It can be noticed that as the self-reported general health decreases, the number of working hours increase with the highest median observed in the ‘Very bad’ health category. The box itself for ‘Very bad’ health category is also placed higher all other boxes, indicating larger numbers of working hours per week among the respondents. As for the other health categories, all four have roughly similar medians. However, the interquartile ranges differ, and it is especially visible for ‘Bad’ health group where the median seem to be very close to Q1, showing skewness of data to the left. Health groups ‘Very Good’ and ‘Fair’ show higher variance in comparison to the group of respondents assessed their health as ‘Good’.

Summary of findings

The results of the analysis are only partially definitive of the relationship between working life and health and Italy. We found out that the amount of working hours a week is connected to the subjective health evaluation among observations, where the poorest health is associated with the number of working hours beyond the standard (=40 hours). We also documented that while in general the subjective health of Italians in the sample is either good or very good, there are fluctuations among different types of organizations, where non-registered organizations (marked as “Other”) and governmental positions are associated with poorer health than in state-owned enterprises or under self-employment. Nevertheless, we couldn’t find any correspondence between different age and alcohol consumption patterns.

It is supposed that a more in-depth analysis should be conducted in order to address the research question with more confidence.
